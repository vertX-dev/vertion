# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume good faith. Argue about code, not about the person
writing it.

## What's expected

This project is a place to build a tool, and everyone taking part should be able
to do that without having to brace themselves first. In practice that means:

- **Criticise the work, not the author.** "This breaks on Windows paths" is
  useful. "Did you even test this?" is not, and says nothing the first sentence
  didn't.
- **Assume the other person is trying.** Most confusion is a missing piece of
  context, not incompetence or bad faith.
- **Accept that people know different things.** A beginner's question is not an
  imposition, and neither is being told you got something wrong.
- **Let disagreements end.** Once a decision is made, it's fine to disagree with
  it; it isn't fine to relitigate it indefinitely.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, whether public or private
- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning comments
- Discriminatory language or behaviour, including about age, body size,
  disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, personal
  appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity or orientation
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Scope and enforcement

The maintainers are responsible for clarifying these standards and will take
what action they judge appropriate and proportionate — which may mean editing or
removing a comment, or blocking an account, depending on severity and on whether
it's a first occurrence.

To report a problem, contact the maintainers privately rather than raising it in
a public thread. On GitHub you can do this by messaging
[@vertX-dev](https://github.com/vertX-dev) directly.
